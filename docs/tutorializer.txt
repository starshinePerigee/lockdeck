Multiple pins
- you don't think all locks are this easy, right? most locks have more than one pin
- each pin has different depths and you're going to have to solve all of them together to unlock the lock
- now so far every pick you've been using has been a pick that only affects a single pin. But most picks are a little to big for that, and some affect a whole lot of pins in one go!

- look at the card - you see those notches? each column on the card represents a pin. So this pick has two columns of effects, which means it can push two pins at once


- you see that stripe on each pick? that red stripe is the "center pin" - it's what happens to the pin you play the pick to.
- picks can affect one pin to the right and three pins to the left of your center pin
- any effects that don't have a pin don't do anything! so for a single-pin lock, you'll only ever get to use the center column. that's why we paint that column red! cuz it's important
- if you get confused, just hover the pick over the pins and look at the preview. it'll tell you what's affecting what
- a final note: test applies across all pins at once

solve this lock without breaking any picks (three-pin lock)




final lesson: jam



 One last thing your picks can do - JAM
- some picks specialize in jam. we call those "wrenches", since they turn the keyway instead of pushing pins directly. Other picks do jam as a side effect. Those aren't called wrenches, but honestly who cares what they're named. I can't even read - just look at the icons.



- oh anyway, jam - jam blocks other effects. If a pin is jammed, each point of jam blocks one point of push. Also, if a pin is jammed, it won't let you test or reveal it.
- it sounds bad, and it can be - but it also has its uses. Take this situation: (set up)
you have a push + 2-reveal pick. if you pushed past the WARNING, you'd be taking a risk. but if you jam first, the push will get absorbed and you can reveal the upcoming depths without moving the pin.
