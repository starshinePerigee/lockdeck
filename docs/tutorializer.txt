11. One last thing your picks can do - JAM
- some picks specialize in jam. we call those "wrenches", since they turn the keyway instead of pushing pins directly. Other picks do jam as a side effect. Those aren't called wrenches, but honestly who cares what they're named. I can't even read - just look at the icons.

- oh anyway, jam - jam blocks other effects. If a pin is jammed, each point of jam blocks one point of push. Also, if a pin is jammed, it won't let you test or reveal it.
- it sounds bad, and it can be - but it also has its uses. Take this situation: (set up)
you have a push + 2-reveal pick. if you pushed past the WARNING, you'd be taking a risk. but if you jam first, the push will get absorbed and you can reveal the upcoming depths without moving the pin.

- you see that stripe on each pick? that red stripe is the "center pin" - it's what happens to the pin you play the pick to.

17. complications
- some depths hinder you without breaking your pick. These test as a yellow "caution". 
- caution depths fall between safe and dangerous depths when it comes to test - if you test three depths, and one tests as caution while the other two are safe, all three show as caution.
- likewise, if you test three depths, and two are caution and one is dangerous, all three show as dangerous.

- your first complication is "slip", a little wobble that makes a pin slip backwards a few depths. Annoying, but not the end of the world.

solve this lock without breaking your pick (slip sends to the beginning)

18. depth activations
- two quirks of locks.
- first: depths can only activate once per turn. you'll have to get around some complicated hazards by activating them, but you can even push through a slip if you have to.
- second: if your pin is resting on a depth that does something, and somehow it didn't activate already, it'll auto-activate all on its own at the end of your next action. Even if all you did was throw a pick away - the lock has a mind of its own
- it might help you to think of it like this: after you do something, every pin activates the depth it's currently at, regardless of if you touched that pin, unless that depth has already activated this turn.
- that matters a lot for the next depth: bounce! this depth moves your pin forward instead of back. this won't break your pin directly, but it can put you in a tight spot
- don't forget you can pull up the depth reference to see all the depths that might be in your lock, and hover over a depth for a quick description of what it does

18. other dangers
- final lesson - there are other depths that can break your pick. you'll see most of them later, but for now, meet spike
- spike is just like break, with two exceptions:
-- first, it's revealed at the start! You always know where spikes are. they're not subtle
-- second, it doesn't have a WARNING depth in front of it. I mean, it might have a warning depth, but that warning is for the BREAK depth specifically, not just "any depth that breaks your pin". So a pin could go spike-warning-break or warning-spike-break. I mean, the spikes are obvious, so this shouldn't be a problem
